# M2 — Kill-test optimizer

Status, 2026-10-13: closed. The gate fails as written and passes in
intent; see Verdict, at the end. Code: `crates/plan`. Parameters:
`data/economy.toml`.

The question (DESIGN.md, Q2): do several development paths (Moon-first,
asteroid-first, Mars-first, Earth-only) each win under plausible draws of the
game's hidden parameters, or does one dominate? For each draw, a linear
program plans the whole 2040–2075 economy with perfect foresight, and a
classifier labels the plan's development path. The gate reads the label
histogram.

## Model

A time-expanded network: six-month periods (72 of them; `--months` sets
the step; the first sweep used quarterly steps), and the nodes of
`data/network.toml`. Earth's surface is not a node: everything from Earth
is bought at LEO at the purchase price plus the launch price of the period.

### Commodities

LOX, methane, equipment, third-party payload, precious metals. Mass in
tonnes, money in $M.

### Transport

- **Edges.** Each fixed leg, in every period, leaving and arriving within the
  period. For each interplanetary route with one end in Earth's system, the
  lowest-Δv transfer leaving in each period, if it costs ≤ 6 km/s; edges that
  another departure on the same route beats on departure, arrival and Δv
  together are dropped. Mars–asteroid and asteroid–asteroid routes are left
  out.
- **Ships.** A class has dry mass D, tank capacity P, cargo capacity C, and
  the edges it may fly (aero legs, interplanetary, which surfaces it lands
  on). Per class and edge the LP has a trip count n (fractional) and cargo by
  commodity. With R = exp(Δv / (g0·Isp)):
  - propellant burned = (R − 1)(D·n + cargo), all loaded at the origin, LOX
    and methane at the mixture ratio;
  - non-propellant cargo ≤ C·n;
  - propellant carried forward + propellant burned ≤ P·n (carried propellant
    rides in the tanks, so a ship can bring its own return propellant).
- **Fleet.** Ships are bought at LEO and conserved: each node holds a fleet,
  which interplanetary trips move between periods. Local trips leave and
  arrive in one period, so a ship can shuttle several times per period; a
  ship-days limit per system and period bounds that: Σ trips × (leg days +
  turnaround) ≤ period days × ships in the system at the start of the period.

### Production

A facility option is a kind at a site. Capacity (t/yr of output) grows when
equipment delivered to the site is installed, after a build delay: each
tonne of equipment adds 1/α t/yr, where α (t of equipment per t/yr) depends
on the kind, the site and the draw (ice grade, solar distance, lunar night).
Production is capacity × period length, and goes into the site's stock.
Asteroid deposits are finite: cumulative production ≤ the body's mass ×
extractable fraction.

### Demand and revenue

- **Third-party payload.** Each demand category has a destination node and a
  stepped demand curve: segments of willingness to pay ($/kg delivered)
  with a quantity (t/yr) that grows with the Earth economy. Delivering
  payload to the destination serves the segments; payload is bought at LEO
  at the launch price.
- **Precious metals** sold at LEO, with a price that falls in steps with
  cumulative tonnes sold.

### Objective and budget

Maximize discounted net value: value of payload served + metal revenue −
Earth purchases (goods + launch) − ship purchases. The Authority's budget
caps capital spending: equipment (purchase + launch) and ships, cumulative
to each year's end ≤ cumulative appropriation + metal revenue. Propellant
and payload launches are operating costs that users pay at cost, so they are
outside the budget.

### What the LP does not capture

- Perfect foresight per draw. The game hides grades until surveyed; an LP
  that knows them overstates the value of asteroids and Phobos. Survey
  costs scaled by 1/p offset this in expectation (see Fixed costs).
- No minimum plant sizes or economies of scale. Fixed costs enter as
  programs, chosen by a heuristic search around the LP (see Fixed costs).
- Free storage, no boil-off, no plant wear or operating costs.
- One transfer per route and period (the cheapest), so no buying speed with
  Δv.
- No crew and no mandates in the base runs.

### Solving

HiGHS, interior point with crossover. The six-month LP has ~51k columns and
~28k rows and solves in ~9 s (~20 s each when 20 run side by side). The
quarterly LP (~97k columns) takes ~30 s and monthly steps (279k columns)
~200 s; all three give the same plants on the central draw. Dual simplex
had not finished after 5 minutes.

## Classification

`crates/plan/src/classify.rs`. Propellant supply over 2040–2060 is split by
source: Earth (LOX and methane bought at LEO), Moon, Mars (surface and
Phobos), asteroids (rated output of plants in each source's system). The
first sweep used 2040–2055; programs delay in-space supply to the late
2040s, so the window grew by five years (`--path-years`).

- **Path:** the in-space sources supplying ≥ 20% of in-space propellant,
  largest first ("Asteroids+Moon"), or "Earth-only" if in-space supply is
  under 25% of all propellant.
- **Opening:** the in-space sources that receive ≥ 20% of the capital
  committed by 2045, largest first: program spending (a technology shared
  by several sources is split by the equipment each installs) plus
  equipment installed, at its price at LEO. The first sweep counted
  equipment alone; with programs, nothing is installed before ~2045, and
  the early decisions are which programs to start. (The order of first
  installation says little: the Moon is reachable at once, asteroids only
  at their windows.)
- **Single-source alternatives** (`--alternatives`): the search is run
  again with the programs of each in-space source alone. Each result's
  gain over Earth-only, as a share of the best plan's gain, says how much
  of the in-space value that source can deliver by itself.
- **Marginal values:** the dual of each node's commodity balance is the
  value of one more tonne there, in $/kg, which is the optimizer's own
  Access Cost Index.

## Gate

From DESIGN.md: pass if no single path wins a large majority of plausible
draws, and first-five-year decisions differ across draws.

## First results (2026-10-13)

100 random draws (seeds 1–100), quarterly steps, all solved to optimality.

| Development path (to 2055) | Draws |
|---|---|
| Asteroids | 44 |
| Asteroids+Moon | 37 |
| Moon | 7 |
| Moon+Asteroids | 3 |
| Earth-only | 3 |
| Mars-led (all via Phobos) | 3 |
| other mixes with Mars | 3 |

Asteroids are the largest in-space source in 83 draws and get the most
opening equipment in 88. Median asteroid share of in-space propellant: 77%.

**Gate: fails as configured.** One path wins a large majority, and the
opening is the same in most draws.

What decides a draw:

- Moon-led draws have dear equipment (median $22.6k/kg against $9.1k) and
  rich ice (7.6 wt% against 4.8). An asteroid plant needs ~2× the equipment
  of a lunar ice plant per t/yr, so dear equipment favors the Moon.
- Earth-only draws have equipment near $100k/kg.
- Mars leads only when Phobos is wet, and then by mining Phobos for the
  cislunar market (seed 85: most Phobos propellant goes to LEO and EML1).
  The Mars surface supplies little: in the central draw (Phobos dry) it is
  not built, and even with its plant mass cut 10× it reaches 13% of supply.
  Nothing near Mars needs much propellant.
- Metals pay only in the rich tail of the grade draws: at ~20 ppm PGM the
  mine needs ~300 t of equipment per t/yr, consistent with Hein et al.
  2020. (Corrected after the second sweep: this first said metals never
  pay, from the central draw. Seed 15 mines Itokawa at α 11 and sells
  ~1,100 t, with or without fixed costs.)
- The budget binds only in the first ~3 years. At these numbers an ISRU
  plant pays for itself in 1–2 years, so the LP builds everything profitable
  at once.

Why asteroids win (central draw: Ryugu, Bennu and 2008 EV5 plus lunar ice):

- They give both LOX and methane; the Moon gives only LOX, so a Moon-led
  plan still buys ~60% of its propellant (all the methane and some LOX)
  from Earth.
- Plant-only equipment is light (0.11 t per t/yr at central values).
- Haulers return by aerocapture into LEO at ~0.1 km/s.
- The LP knows which asteroids are wet and pays no development costs.

One-factor sensitivity on the central draw (path, in-space shares):

| Change | Path |
|---|---|
| none | Asteroids+Moon (asteroids 64% of all supply, Moon 20%) |
| asteroid plant equipment ×2 | Moon+Asteroids (Moon 31%, asteroids 12%) |
| asteroid plant equipment ×3, ×5, ×10 | Moon (asteroids 0%) |
| lunar plant equipment ×0.3 or ×2 | Asteroids(+Moon), barely changed |
| launch price ×0.5 | Moon+Asteroids |
| launch price ×0.3 | Moon |
| launch price ×2, ×4 | Asteroids |
| Mars plant equipment ×0.1 | Asteroids+Moon, Mars 13% |

The winner flips over a factor of 2 in asteroid plant mass: a knife edge,
as an LP without fixed costs would give.

## Fixed costs

The first sweep let the optimizer build any plant anywhere from 2040, with
no development cost and with free knowledge of every deposit. This section
adds what a real program pays before its first plant.

### Programs

A *program* is a fixed cost that must be paid before some plants can be
installed:

- **Technology** programs, one per plant technology: lunar ice, regolith
  oxygen (polar and mare sites), Mars ice, volatiles (asteroids and Phobos),
  precious metals. Cost F (drawn per game) and duration L.
- **Survey** programs, one per deposit whose content is uncertain: lunar
  polar ice, Mars ice, Phobos, each asteroid. Regolith needs no survey.
  Cost S / p, where p is the prior probability that the deposit has the
  resource (Phobos 0.4, a C-type asteroid 0.85 for volatiles, an
  unclassified one 0.3). The optimizer still knows which deposits are rich,
  so it is charged the expected cost of finding one: 1/p surveys of the
  class per success. That removes its information advantage in expectation.
  (Some deposits will be known by 2040 and need no luck: Ryugu and Bennu
  are hydrated per their returned samples, and JAXA's MMX should settle
  Phobos by ~2031. Not modeled yet.)

A plant needs its technology program and its survey (if any). A program
spends at most F / L per year, so it takes at least L years, and its plants
may be installed once it is fully paid. Program spending counts against the
capital budget.

### LP

Per program j: a selection column y_j (fixed to 0 or 1 per solve) and
spending columns g_jt ≥ 0 with Σ_t g_jt = F_j y_j, g_jt ≤ F_j dt / L_j, and
g_jt = 0 from the period its plants may first be installed. Install columns
of a plant are fixed to zero before that period, and in every period if any
of its programs is unselected. Spending enters the objective (discounted)
and the budget rows.

### Search

Choosing the programs is a fixed-charge problem; a MIP of this size is out
of reach (dual simplex alone takes minutes). Instead, dynamic slope scaling
(Kim & Pardalos 1999). Every solve is a fresh interior-point solve (~9 s):
simplex warm-started from the previous basis measured slower (7–45 s).

1. Solve with every program open from its earliest date (2040 + L) and
   free.
2. Charge each program's cost per tonne of equipment installed at its
   plants: c_j = PV(F_j) / U_j, where U_j is the discounted tonnage the
   previous solve installed there and PV(F_j) is the cost discounted to a
   just-in-time start before the first installation. Re-solve. A program
   that falls out of use keeps its last charge.
3. Stop when the set of programs in use repeats and each one's usage moved
   by less than 20% (at most 8 solves).
4. Exact solve: fund the programs in use, each ready at its first
   installation in the last solve, and close the rest. Compare with
   Earth-only, then try dropping each funded program (with any surveys
   that only it made useful); keep a drop that improves the exact value.

At the fixed point the linear charges equal the fixed costs, so step 4's
exact value is close to step 3's; the drops catch programs that the linear
charges kept too cheaply.

### Time step

Six-month steps from here on: on the central draw they give the same four
plants as quarterly steps (capacities within ~15%, objective within 1.2%)
in 10 s instead of 29 s.

### Results (2026-10-13)

100 random draws (seeds 1–100, the same hidden parameters per seed as the
first sweep), six-month steps, all solved to optimality; ~9 LP solves per
draw for the best plan, ~25 with `--alternatives` (68 min for the sweep at
20 jobs). Costs and durations as in `data/economy.toml`: median program
spending per draw $4.7B (quartiles $2.5–6.5B), against a median in-space
value (best plan minus Earth-only) of $50B ($25–102B).

| Largest in-space source (propellant to 2060) | No fixed costs | Programs |
|---|---|---|
| Asteroids | 84 | 75 |
| Moon | 12 | 18 |
| Mars | 4 | 2 |
| none (no in-space supply) | 0 | 5 |

Path labels with programs: Asteroids 38, Asteroids+Moon 32, Moon 12,
Earth-only 10, mixes with Mars 6, Moon+Asteroids 2. (No fixed costs, same
window: Asteroids 47, Asteroids+Moon 33, Earth-only 6, Moon 5, others 9.)

Supply shares understate the Moon: on the central draw asteroids supply
twice the Moon's propellant, yet each source alone keeps a similar share
of the in-space value (Moon 69%, asteroids 74%). So the gate is read in
value too, from the single-source alternatives:

- **Best single source:** asteroids in 69 draws, Moon 24, Mars 2; 5 draws
  have under $1B of in-space value.
- **One source usually dominates within a draw.** The best single source
  alone keeps a median 94% of the in-space value; combining sources adds a
  median 6% (upper quartile 18%). The runner-up alone keeps a median 39%,
  and comes within 20 percentage points of the best in only 13 of 95
  draws.
- **Moon-best draws have rich ice** (median 6.8 wt% against 4.6). Equipment
  price no longer separates them (median $10.1k/kg against $9.2k): every
  path now pays similar program costs.
- **Early decisions differ.** Sources receiving over 5% of the capital
  committed by 2045: Moon and asteroids 46 draws, asteroids alone 24, Moon
  alone 19, nothing 5, Mars with others 6. Technology programs funded:
  lunar ice and volatiles 51, volatiles 23, lunar ice 13, none 5, plus
  metals 5, regolith oxygen 2, Mars ice 1.
- **Lead times cost more than program costs.** On the central draw the
  plan without programs is worth $260.9B; programs open from 2040 + L but
  free, $241.4B; the exact plan, $236.8B. Delay costs ~$19.5B and spending
  ~$4.7B. In-space supply starts ~2046, so Earth's share of propellant to
  2060 rises from a median 11% to 28%.

**Gate.** First-five-year decisions now differ across draws (no early
commitment set above 46%): that half passes. The path half still fails:
asteroids are the best source in ~70% of draws by supply and by value,
the Moon in ~20–25%, Mars almost never.

Still unmodeled, mostly in asteroids' favor: plant upkeep (spares shipped
to remote sites), survey failure, composition priors (the 0.85 for C-types
is above the literature's 0.25–0.7: Rivkin 2012, Fornasier et al. 2014,
Elvis 2014), and the search is a heuristic (on the central draw the
single-source searches agree with the drop solves).

## Propellant chemistry test (2026-10-13)

Hypothesis: asteroids win because they give both LOX and methane, while
lunar ice gives only LOX under the methalox-only rule (DESIGN.md). Test:
`--fuel lunar-ice` (removed once both propellants were modeled) counted
the ice plant's output as complete propellant at the methalox mixture, a
stand-in for ships that burn the ice's hydrogen
(water splits 8:1, hydrolox engines run ~6:1, so ice yields complete
propellant plus spare oxygen). Same 100 draws, programs as above.

| Largest in-space source (propellant to 2060) | Methalox only | Lunar ice complete |
|---|---|---|
| Moon | 18 | 84 |
| Asteroids | 75 | 12 |
| Mars | 2 | 1 |
| none | 5 | 3 |

- Paths: Moon 68, Moon+Asteroids 14, Asteroids 6, Earth-only 6,
  Asteroids+Moon 5, Mars 1. Openings: Moon 81.
- Technologies funded: lunar ice alone 61, lunar ice and volatiles 25,
  volatiles alone 6, none 3, with metals 5.
- Median in-space value (best minus Earth-only) rises from $50B to $118B.
  On the central draw the best plan is Moon-only ($309B against $237B),
  with a ~217 kt/yr ice plant; asteroids alone would give $226B.
- The remaining asteroid-led draws have dearer equipment (median
  $12.7k/kg against $8.6k); ice grade does not separate them.

The stand-in is an upper bound for the Moon: it lets lunar propellant fuel
every ship and third-party buyer, including methalox haulers bound for
Mars and asteroids, and ignores hydrogen's boil-off and bulky tanks (and
its higher Isp). A model with both chemistries, each ship class burning
one, would land between the two columns. Either way, the propellant rule
is a design lever that decides which source leads, ahead of any cost
parameter swept so far.

## Two propellants

DESIGN.md now has methalox and hydrolox (decided 2026-10-13). In the LP:

- **Commodities:** LOX, methane and hydrogen. Earth sells all three at
  LEO. Lunar ice yields LOX and hydrogen 8:1 (all of the water), so its
  grade is now water per regolith mass; Mars, Phobos and asteroid plants
  yield methalox as before.
- **Ships** each burn one propellant, with its exhaust velocity and
  mixture: the burn draws LOX and that fuel at the origin. A ship's tanks
  hold LOX and its own fuel; the other fuel travels in the hold.
- **Boil-off:** hydrogen stocks keep (1 − b)^days of their mass from one
  period to the next, and hydrogen cargo arrives with (1 − b)^days of what
  left. Methane and LOX are taken as storable.
- **Demand:** the hub's propellant buyers split between the propellants by
  fixed shares (one draw of the demand level, so seeds keep their other
  parameters).
- **Ship classes:** the hauler and the lander burn methalox; a hydrolox
  lander and an aerobraking hydrolox tug join them.

Parameters (sourced in `data/economy.toml`): hydrolox 450 s at O/F 6;
hydrogen boil-off median 0.05%/day (0.01–0.2); hydrolox lander 30 t dry,
150 t propellant, 50 t cargo; aerobraking tug 18 / 150 / 50; the hub's
buyers split 50/50. The lunar ice plant is now counted per tonne of water
(0.035 t of equipment per t/yr at central values; studies quote LOX+LH2,
~1.4 t of water per t).

|---|---|---|---|
| Moon | 18 | 84 | 88 |
| Asteroids | 75 | 12 | 12 |
| Mars | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| none | 5 | 3 | 0 |

- Paths: Moon 76, Moon+Asteroids 9, Asteroids 8, Asteroids+Moon 4,
  Earth-only 2, Moon+Mars 1. Openings: Moon 80.
- Technologies funded: lunar ice alone 53, lunar ice and volatiles 33 (5
  more with metals), volatiles alone 6, with Mars ice 2. Where asteroids
  are funded beside the Moon, dropping them loses a median 4% of the
  in-space value (upper quartile 15%): a supplement, mostly of methane.
- Asteroid-led draws have poor lunar ice (median 3.7 wt% against 5.4) and
  dearer equipment ($12.3k/kg against $10.8k).
- Median in-space value $108B (methalox only: $50B).
- Central draw: Moon only, worth $364B against $222B Earth-only; asteroids
  alone would add 17% of that gain. The ice plant reaches ~315 kt/yr of
  water. Hydrolox landers (41) and tugs (52) carry the cislunar traffic;
  Earth supplies the light fuels, methane for methalox buyers and hydrogen
  to burn the spare lunar oxygen.

Why the Moon wins: it is days away at any time, and its oxygen (78–86% of
either propellant) serves both. Asteroids' unique product is methane, 22%
of methalox, which Earth launches at $100–250/kg.

The LP scales the lunar plant linearly to ~100× the largest study
(Kornuta et al. 2019: 2,450 t/yr of water) at one site, with no limit on
rich ice: cold-trap deposits are patchy and finite, which the model does
not see.

**Gate:** fails on both halves; one source (the Moon) leads 88% of draws
and gets the most early capital in 80.

## Lunar ice supply curve

A plant row may list **deposits**: each is its own plant option with a
grade (the site's drawn grade times a fixed factor, so equipment per t/yr
rises as grade falls) and a drawn recoverable size, which the LP enforces
as a reserve. The deposits of one row share its technology program and
one survey. Reserves are sampled after every other parameter, so seeds
keep their other values.

South-pole deposits (`data/economy.toml`, synthesis of LWIMS, Li et al.
2018, Hayne et al. 2021 and Brown et al. 2022; no published tonnage by
grade exists): rich at the drawn grade (5.6 wt% central), 11 Mt of
recoverable water; typical at 0.54× the grade, 34 Mt; lean at 0.18×, 60
Mt; each lognormal with σ 1.0–1.3 (one to two orders of magnitude). The
grade dependence of the plant was refit to Kleinhenz & Paz's system mass
and power at 1–10 wt% (power at 0.15 t/kWe): 0.026 + 0.0005/grade t per
t/yr of water, so 1 wt% ice needs ~2.2× the equipment of 5.6 wt% (the old
split gave ~3.3×).

### Results (2026-10-13)

| Largest in-space source | Unlimited rich ice | Deposit tiers |
|---|---|---|
| Moon | 88 | 90 |
| Asteroids | 12 | 10 |

- Paths: Moon 81, Moon+Asteroids 7, Asteroids+Moon 7, Asteroids 3,
  Earth-only 1, Moon+Mars 1; 84 draws keep their path.
- The central draw does not reach past the rich deposit: ~315 kt/yr from
  2046 is ~9.5 Mt by 2075, under its 11 Mt.
- Where the rich deposit is small, the plan moves to leaner lunar ice, not
  to asteroids: the worst draws lose up to 35% of their in-space value and
  stay Moon-led. (The refit grade dependence also lowers equipment for
  lean ice, which raises value in other draws: the change in best value
  has quartiles −10%, −1%, +14% of the in-space value.)

The Moon's lead holds under the literature's supply limits: the lean tiers
cost only 1.2–2.2× the rich one's equipment, and are still days away at
any time.

## Verdict (2026-10-13)

M2 is closed. The gate fails as written and passes in intent.

- **Fails as written.** Under one set of rules, one source leads: with
  both propellants and deposit tiers, the Moon is the largest in-space
  source in 90 of 100 draws and gets the most early capital in 93 (the
  only major recipient in 81).
- **Passes in intent.** Orbital mechanics and sourced costs do not fix the
  answer; the rules do. With methalox as the only propellant and otherwise
  the same sourced numbers, asteroids lead 75 of 100. Both rules are
  defensible, so which path dominates is a design setting.
- **Later decisions differ by world.** 47 draws fund more than lunar ice
  (asteroid volatiles 44, metals 6, Mars ice 2); the asteroid surveyed
  varies (Bennu 24, Ryugu 20, 2008 EV5 14, four others once to three
  times); the 100 draws use 23 distinct sets of programs.
- **Foresight.** The optimizer sees every hidden draw; the player will
  not. Grades and deposit sizes stay hidden until surveyed, so how a player
  hedges is a question for play, not for M2.

Settings that move the opening, for tuning (DESIGN.md, Open questions):

- **Whether minable lunar ice exists.** It always does here; only its
  grade and size vary (Phobos volatiles exist with probability 0.4). With a
  probability below one, the opening survey becomes a gamble with asteroids
  as the fallback.
- **Demand mix:** the methalox/hydrolox split at the hub, and deep-space
  mandates such as a Mars program.
- **Hydrogen boil-off.**
- **Asteroid water as a source of hydrolox.**
