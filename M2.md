# M2 — Kill-test optimizer

Status: first sweep done, 2026-10-13. Code: `crates/plan`. Parameters:
`data/economy.toml`.

The question (DESIGN.md, Q2): do several development paths (Moon-first,
asteroid-first, Mars-first, Earth-only) each win under plausible draws of the
game's hidden parameters, or does one dominate? For each draw, a linear
program plans the whole 2040–2075 economy with perfect foresight, and a
classifier labels the plan's development path. The gate reads the label
histogram.

## Model

A time-expanded network: quarterly periods (144 of them; `--months` sets
the step), and the nodes of `data/network.toml`. Earth's surface is not a node: everything from Earth is
bought at LEO at the purchase price plus the launch price of the period.

### Commodities

LOX, methane, equipment, third-party payload, precious metals. Mass in
tonnes, money in $M.

### Transport

- **Edges.** Each fixed leg, in every period, leaving and arriving within the
  period. For each interplanetary route with one end in Earth's system, the
  lowest-Δv transfer leaving in each period, if it costs ≤ 6 km/s; edges that
  another departure on the same route beats on departure, arrival and Δv
  together are dropped. Mars–asteroid and asteroid–asteroid routes are left
  out.
- **Ships.** A class has dry mass D, tank capacity P, cargo capacity C, and
  the edges it may fly (aero legs, interplanetary, which surfaces it lands
  on). Per class and edge the LP has a trip count n (fractional) and cargo by
  commodity. With R = exp(Δv / (g0·Isp)):
  - propellant burned = (R − 1)(D·n + cargo), all loaded at the origin, LOX
    and methane at the mixture ratio;
  - non-propellant cargo ≤ C·n;
  - propellant carried forward + propellant burned ≤ P·n (carried propellant
    rides in the tanks, so a ship can bring its own return propellant).
- **Fleet.** Ships are bought at LEO and conserved: each node holds a fleet,
  which interplanetary trips move between periods. Local trips leave and
  arrive in one period, so a ship can shuttle several times per period; a
  ship-days limit per system and period bounds that: Σ trips × (leg days +
  turnaround) ≤ period days × ships in the system at the start of the period.

### Production

A facility option is a kind at a site. Capacity (t/yr of output) grows when
equipment delivered to the site is installed, after a build delay: each
tonne of equipment adds 1/α t/yr, where α (t of equipment per t/yr) depends
on the kind, the site and the draw (ice grade, solar distance, lunar night).
Production is capacity × period length, and goes into the site's stock.
Asteroid deposits are finite: cumulative production ≤ the body's mass ×
extractable fraction.

### Demand and revenue

- **Third-party payload.** Each demand category has a destination node and a
  stepped demand curve: segments of willingness to pay ($/kg delivered)
  with a quantity (t/yr) that grows with the Earth economy. Delivering
  payload to the destination serves the segments; payload is bought at LEO
  at the launch price.
- **Precious metals** sold at LEO, with a price that falls in steps with
  cumulative tonnes sold.

### Objective and budget

Maximize discounted net value: value of payload served + metal revenue −
Earth purchases (goods + launch) − ship purchases. The Authority's budget
caps capital spending: equipment (purchase + launch) and ships, cumulative
to each year's end ≤ cumulative appropriation + metal revenue. Propellant
and payload launches are operating costs that users pay at cost, so they are
outside the budget.

### What the LP does not capture

- Perfect foresight per draw. The game hides grades until surveyed; an LP
  that knows them overstates the value of asteroids and Phobos.
- No fixed costs or minimum plant sizes (it is an LP, not a MIP), so no
  economies of scale and no lumpy commitment.
- Free storage, no boil-off, no plant wear or operating costs.
- One transfer per route and period (the cheapest), so no buying speed with
  Δv.
- No crew and no mandates in the base runs.

### Solving

HiGHS, interior point with crossover. The quarterly LP has ~97k columns and
~53k rows and solves in ~30 s (several minutes each when 16 run side by
side); dual simplex had not finished after 5 minutes. Monthly steps (279k
columns) take ~200 s and give the same plan for the central draw: the same
four plants, capacities within ~15%, objective within 1.5%.

## Classification

`crates/plan/src/classify.rs`. Propellant supply over 2040–2055 is split by
source: Earth (LOX and methane bought at LEO), Moon, Mars (surface and
Phobos), asteroids (rated output of plants in each source's system).

- **Path:** the in-space sources supplying ≥ 20% of in-space propellant,
  largest first ("Asteroids+Moon"), or "Earth-only" if in-space supply is
  under 25% of all propellant.
- **Opening:** the in-space sources that receive ≥ 20% of the equipment
  installed by 2045, largest first. (The order of first installation says
  little: the Moon is reachable at once, asteroids only at their windows.)
- **Marginal values:** the dual of each node's commodity balance is the
  value of one more tonne there, in $/kg, which is the optimizer's own
  Access Cost Index.

## Gate

From DESIGN.md: pass if no single path wins a large majority of plausible
draws, and first-five-year decisions differ across draws.

## First results (2026-10-13)

100 random draws (seeds 1–100), quarterly steps, all solved to optimality.

| Development path (to 2055) | Draws |
|---|---|
| Asteroids | 44 |
| Asteroids+Moon | 37 |
| Moon | 7 |
| Moon+Asteroids | 3 |
| Earth-only | 3 |
| Mars-led (all via Phobos) | 3 |
| other mixes with Mars | 3 |

Asteroids are the largest in-space source in 83 draws and get the most
opening equipment in 88. Median asteroid share of in-space propellant: 77%.

**Gate: fails as configured.** One path wins a large majority, and the
opening is the same in most draws.

What decides a draw:

- Moon-led draws have dear equipment (median $22.6k/kg against $9.1k) and
  rich ice (7.6 wt% against 4.8). An asteroid plant needs ~2× the equipment
  of a lunar ice plant per t/yr, so dear equipment favors the Moon.
- Earth-only draws have equipment near $100k/kg.
- Mars leads only when Phobos is wet, and then by mining Phobos for the
  cislunar market (seed 85: most Phobos propellant goes to LEO and EML1).
  The Mars surface supplies little: in the central draw (Phobos dry) it is
  not built, and even with its plant mass cut 10× it reaches 13% of supply.
  Nothing near Mars needs much propellant.
- Metals never pay: at ~20 ppm PGM the mine needs ~300 t of equipment per
  t/yr, consistent with Hein et al. 2020.
- The budget binds only in the first ~3 years. At these numbers an ISRU
  plant pays for itself in 1–2 years, so the LP builds everything profitable
  at once.

Why asteroids win (central draw: Ryugu, Bennu and 2008 EV5 plus lunar ice):

- They give both LOX and methane; the Moon gives only LOX, so a Moon-led
  plan still buys ~60% of its propellant (all the methane and some LOX)
  from Earth.
- Plant-only equipment is light (0.11 t per t/yr at central values).
- Haulers return by aerocapture into LEO at ~0.1 km/s.
- The LP knows which asteroids are wet and pays no development costs.

One-factor sensitivity on the central draw (path, in-space shares):

| Change | Path |
|---|---|
| none | Asteroids+Moon (asteroids 64% of all supply, Moon 20%) |
| asteroid plant equipment ×2 | Moon+Asteroids (Moon 31%, asteroids 12%) |
| asteroid plant equipment ×3, ×5, ×10 | Moon (asteroids 0%) |
| lunar plant equipment ×0.3 or ×2 | Asteroids(+Moon), barely changed |
| launch price ×0.5 | Moon+Asteroids |
| launch price ×0.3 | Moon |
| launch price ×2, ×4 | Asteroids |
| Mars plant equipment ×0.1 | Asteroids+Moon, Mars 13% |

The winner flips over a factor of 2 in asteroid plant mass: a knife edge,
as an LP without fixed costs would give.
